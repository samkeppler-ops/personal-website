✏️ A framework for business education

Material-Based
Thinking

Four actions, done away from the screen.

By Samantha Keppler, from the Material School Initiative at Michigan Ross · September 2026

Material-Based Thinking (MBT) is a critical part of the learning process, in classrooms and in the field. Understanding is enhanced and outcomes improve when MBT is part of the process. It is best done in technology-free spaces but can be done whenever humans have technology-free periods of time. It is meant to be done in sequence with technology and AI use, either before, after, or in between.

MBT involves four actions

🙋

Action one

Asking

Asking questions of teammates, customers, or other humans who have relevant information and experiences to the problem or question at hand. It means preferring open questions to ones that invite a yes or no, and treating the first answer as the beginning rather than the end of an exchange. Good asking is specific, unhurried, and willing to look uninformed in front of someone who knows more.

👂

Action two

Listening

Listening to the information and experiences shared by others. It includes letting an answer change the question that follows, noticing what a person does not say or declines to say, and recording their words rather than a paraphrase already shaped toward a conclusion. Notes taken by hand help here, because the slower pace forces selection and keeps attention on the speaker.

✏️

Action three

Drawing or Building

Using human brains and hands to draw non-digital diagrams, make lists or tables, or build prototypes from available materials — paper, whiteboards, sticky notes, index cards, cardboard, tape, string, blocks, and the actual objects under study. The output might be a process map, a floor layout, a decision table, a sketch of an interface, or a working physical mock-up.

👣

Action four

Moving

Moving the body through physical spaces, both to collect information through observation and to reflect on current thinking. This includes site visits, tracing a single unit from one end of a process to the other, or walking meetings where a problem is discussed away from a table and a screen.
