Material

Giving kids a material education in an AI world. 🎨📚

Material School combines rigorous research with the experiences of district leaders, teachers, and parents to enable meaningful, material learning experiences in an AI world.

🎥 Watch the episodes 🧮 Try the calculators

Three pillars

🎒

Smarter supply choices

Understanding which supplies and materials students, teachers, and schools most need.

🚚

Efficient procurement

Getting those materials where they're needed quickly and affordably.

🧩

Technology supports

Using technology and AI — often behind the scenes by adults — to strengthen hands-on, material learning.

Cell phone bans

Nearly every state requires schools to ban phones — but how is not clear.

🎤 Guest Speaker · 25 min

Cell Phone Bans

Monica Kress has run three versions of a ban across her schools. She explains the pros and cons of each.
📋 Efficiency Eval · 4 min

Lockers vs. Pouches

Why pouches can be really efficient for fast dismissals with lots of students, and when backpacks are not enough.

📱 Then put your own numbers in the Cell Phone Ban Plan Calculator.

📝

AI for IEPs

About 15% of students have an IEP and managing IEPs is resource-intensive. AI looks like the obvious fix, and it isn't that simple.

🎤 Guest Speaker · 20 min

AI for IEPs

Allison Hite, an attorney who advises districts, on what student data privacy law actually prohibits — and where AI still can help.
📋 Efficiency Eval · 4 min

Stop Asking It to "Make For Me"

Using AI for general advice or summaries can avoid the privacy restrictions. Can still save time without the violation.
🔬

Elementary science kits

Forty-nine states expect science in every elementary grade. Meeting that takes physical stuff — seeds, soil, bulbs, wires — and somebody has to buy and maintain it.

🎤 Guest Speaker · 26 min

Elementary Science Kits

Betsy Davis, who chaired the National Academies committee on elementary science, on what teaching science materially needs.
📋 Efficiency Eval · 5 min

The Trade-off Districts Are Actually Making

Science kits are expensive but can save teacher and staff effort cobbling together resources. When are they worth it?
